# Writing & Communication Guidelines

> [!NOTE]
> **Context Adaptation:** Communication is only effective when its tone matches the medium and the audience. Never write a casual chat message like an academic paper, and never write an executive proposal like a casual chat.

---

## 1. Context-Adaptive Writing Matrix

| Medium | Tone & Posture | Sentence Structure | Formatting Rules | What to Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **WhatsApp / Instant Messaging** | Conversational, direct, authentic, concise | Short fragments, natural cadence | Single-line breaks, sparing emojis, bulleted lists for plans | Robotic corporate speak, formal salutations ("Dear Sir"), essay-length paragraphs |
| **Email (Work / Business)** | Professional, courteous, actionable | Clear, active voice sentences | BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front), bolded deadlines/requests | Burying the ask at the bottom, vague subject lines |
| **Executive Proposals & Reports** | Authoritative, strategic, data-grounded | Structured compound sentences | Executive summary, bold callouts, numbered recommendations | Hand-wavy adjectives, unsubstantiated claims, flowery prose |
| **Academic & Scholarly Writing** | Objective, analytical, rigorously neutral | Complex, precise, evidence-linked | Academic sections (Abstract, Methodology, Discussion), citations | First-person casual opinions, colloquialisms, rhetorical questions |
| **Social Media Captions** | Engaging, hook-driven, personable | Punchy opening line, rhythmic spacing | Micro-paragraphs (1–2 sentences each), strong closing question | Cluttered hashtag walls, generic AI intros ("In today's fast-paced world...") |

## 2. Channel-Specific Execution Standards

### 2.1 WhatsApp & Direct Messaging
- **The 3-Second Read:** Keep messages self-contained. The recipient should understand the point without scrolling.
- **Natural Rhythm:** Write like a thoughtful human being. Use punctuation naturally; avoid overly stiff sentence capitalization if the context is quick coordination.
- **Coordination Formatting:** When proposing times or items, use quick single-line breaks:
  ```text
  Hey! Quick check on tomorrow:
  - 10:30 AM or 2:00 PM work better for you?
  - Need me to bring the finalized draft?
  ```

### 2.2 Business Emails (The BLUF Protocol)
- **Subject Line:** State the project, action, and urgency:
  - *Bad:* `Update`
  - *Good:* `[Action Required] Project Alpha: Sign-off needed by Friday 3 PM`
- **Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF):** Open with the conclusion, decision, or request in the very first sentence. Provide background context *after* the request, not before.
- **The Single Call to Action:** End with an explicit next step: "Could you confirm approval by Thursday 5 PM?"

### 2.3 Executive Proposals & Business Reports
- **Structure:**
  1. **Executive Summary:** The problem, recommended solution, and expected outcome in $\le 200$ words.
  2. **The Current Problem & Cost of Inaction:** Why solving this now matters.
  3. **Proposed Strategy & Deliverables:** Concrete milestones and deliverables.
  4. **Timeline & Resource Investment:** Transparent schedule and budget.
  5. **Risk Mitigation:** Anticipated roadblocks and countermeasures.

### 2.4 Academic & Research Papers
- **Evidence-First Assertions:** Never make a declarative claim without linking to empirical data, a theoretical framework, or a published source.
- **Hedging & Precision:** Use academic hedging appropriately (`suggests`, `indicates`, `the evidence points toward`) rather than absolute generalizations (`proves beyond doubt`).
- **Third-Person Neutrality:** Maintain objective detachment.

## 3. Editing & Proofreading Protocol

Run all writing through this 4-pass editing filter:
1. **Pass 1 — The Cut (Fluff Elimination):** Delete redundant filler words (`in order to` $\rightarrow$ `to`, `at this point in time` $\rightarrow$ `now`, `due to the fact that` $\rightarrow$ `because`).
2. **Pass 2 — Rhythm & Sentence Variety:** Alternate sentence lengths. Follow a long descriptive sentence with a short, punchy one. If three sentences in a row have identical length, rewrite one.
3. **Pass 3 — Active Voice Conversion:** Replace passive constructions with active subjects (*"The decision was made by the team"* $\rightarrow$ *"The team decided"*).
4. **Pass 4 — AI Cliché Cleansing:** Search and eradicate telltale AI phrases:
   - "Delve into", "Tapestry", "Beacon", "Testament to", "In today's digital landscape", "Furthermore", "Moreover", "It is crucial to remember".
