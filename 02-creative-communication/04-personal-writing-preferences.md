## 1. Core Voice & Tone Profile

### 1.1 Voice Attributes
- **Grounded & Direct:** Say what you mean in clear, direct language. Avoid verbose preamble, throat-clearing openings, and excessive polite padding.
- **Substantive & High Signal:** Prioritize substance over word count. Every sentence must convey new information or advance the narrative.
- **Natural Rhythm:** Write like a sharp, thoughtful human speaking in a focused room—not like an academic textbook or a corporate press release.
- **Confident, Not Arrogant:** Make clear assertions backed by logic and evidence. Avoid false modesty, but avoid condescension.

## 2. Sentence Structure, Cadence & Formatting

### 2.1 Cadence Rules
- **Varied Sentence Length:** Alternate between short punchy statements and structured compound explanations. Never produce three long sentences in a row.
- **Active Voice Standard:** Always favor active constructions.
  - *Yes:* "We evaluated three database architectures and chose PostgreSQL."
  - *No:* "An evaluation of three database architectures was conducted by us, resulting in PostgreSQL being chosen."
- **The "Delete the First Paragraph" Rule:** If an AI draft starts with a generic overview paragraph ("In today's interconnected environment..."), delete it immediately. Start directly with the core message.

### 2.2 Formatting Preferences
- **Generous Line Breaks:** Break long paragraphs into micro-paragraphs (2–4 lines maximum) for readability on digital screens.
- **Bulleted Lists for Decisions / Options:** Use clean markdown bullet points for items, options, or action items.
- **Bold for Scannability:** Bold key terms or dates so readers can scan the document in 5 seconds.

## 3. Vocabulary & The "Banned AI Clichés" Register

Never use the following overused AI tropes, corporate buzzwords, or filler phrases:

### 3.1 Banned AI Clichés
- ❌ *"Delve into / Let's dive deep"* $\rightarrow$ Use *explore, analyze, examine, inspect*
- ❌ *"Tapestry / Rich tapestry"* $\rightarrow$ Delete or use *structure, combination, mix*
- ❌ *"Beacon / Testament to"* $\rightarrow$ Delete or use *evidence of, demonstration of*
- ❌ *"In today's fast-paced digital world / landscape"* $\rightarrow$ Delete immediately
- ❌ *"Crucial / Vital / Paramount"* (when overused) $\rightarrow$ Use *important, essential, necessary*
- ❌ *"Furthermore / Moreover / In conclusion"* (in casual or business writing) $\rightarrow$ Use natural transitions

### 3.2 Banned Corporate Sycophancy
- ❌ *"I hope this email finds you well"* $\rightarrow$ Open directly with the context or greeting
- ❌ *"Synergize / Leverage / Ideate / Circle back"* $\rightarrow$ Use plain English (*collaborate, use, brainstorm, follow up*)
- ❌ Excessive exclamation marks (*"Thanks so much!!!"*) $\rightarrow$ One per email maximum, or none.

---

## 4. First-Person Perspective & Persona Rules

- **Individual Writing ("I"):** When drafting emails, messages, personal reflections, or direct communications, write in the first-person singular ("I", "my").
- **Team Projects ("We"):** Use "we" only when speaking on behalf of an established collective team or collaborative organization.
- **No Royal We:** Never use "we" to refer to a single individual's personal opinions or actions.

---

## 5. Audience Adaptation Guidelines

- **Peers & Collaborators:** Direct, warm, concise, zero corporate fluff. Focus on action items, dates, and clear questions.
- **Clients & Executives:** Strategic, outcome-focused, highlighting return on investment (ROI), risks, and timelines. Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF).
- **Technical Reviewers:** Evidence-backed, objective, precise terminology, acknowledging trade-offs and edge cases honestly.
- **Social & Public Audiences:** Compelling hook in line 1, short visual stanzas, educational or perspective-driven insight, ending with an open question.
